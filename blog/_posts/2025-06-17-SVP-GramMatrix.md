## Problem Statement

The Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) is a well-known problem in lattice theory and computational complexity. It asks for the shortest non-zero vector in a lattice, given a basis for that lattice. The Gram matrix of a lattice is a symmetric matrix that captures the inner products of the basis vectors. The problem of minimizing the Gram matrix is closely related to finding the shortest vector in the lattice.

## Equivalence

The Gram matrix $$ G $$ of a lattice with basis vectors $$ b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_n $$ is defined as:

$$
G = \begin{pmatrix}
\langle b_i, b_j \rangle \end{pmatrix}_{1 \leq i, j \leq n}
$$

where $$ \langle b_i, b_j \rangle $$ is the inner product of the vectors $$ b_i $$ and $$ b_j $$.

The shortest vector problem can be formulated as finding a non-zero vector $$ v $$ such that:

$$
\|v\|^2 = \langle v, v \rangle = \min_{v \in L \setminus \{0\}} \langle v, v \rangle
$$

where $$ L $$ is the lattice generated by the basis vectors.

The Gram matrix $$ G $$ can be used to express the squared norm of any vector $$ v $$ in the lattice as:

$$
\|v\|^2 = v^T G v
$$

We want to show that minimizing the Gram matrix is equivalent to minimizing the squared norm of the shortest vector in the lattice.

## Quantum Computing Context

This equivalence is particularly useful in the context of quantum computing. By leveraging quantum annealing, we can solve the SVP by minimizing the Gram matrix. Quantum annealing provides a framework to efficiently explore the solution space, especially when the problem is formulated as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem. This approach opens up new possibilities for solving lattice problems using quantum techniques.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the problem of finding the shortest vector in a lattice (SVP) is equivalent to minimizing the Gram matrix of that lattice. This equivalence allows us to use techniques from linear algebra, optimization, and quantum computing to solve the SVP efficiently in certain cases.
